The journal is transparent about the level of review each article has received,
and every article carries a visible status label (see Article status). At
present the journal performs editorial review only: the editors screen
submissions for scope, technical correctness, the soundness of the stated threat
model, the adequacy and verifiability of citations, and reproducibility where
applicable. The journal does not currently operate external peer review, and no
article is described as peer reviewed. If a formal peer-review process is later
adopted, it will be documented here and reflected in the status labels.

## Article status

Each article is labeled with its publication status, and that label is shown on
the article and recorded in its metadata:

- Draft -- posted for comment; not peer reviewed and subject to change.
- Published (version of record) -- the stable, citable version; editorial review
  only, not peer reviewed.
- Corrected -- a published version of record in which errors have been
  corrected; see the correction notice on the article.

## Publication ethics

Authors must disclose conflicts of interest and may not submit work under review
elsewhere. Plagiarism and fabricated or falsified results are grounds for
rejection or retraction.

## Corrections and retractions

Published articles are kept at stable URLs and are not silently altered; URLs are
never changed. Substantive errors are addressed by a dated correction notice on
the article, which is then labeled "Corrected" and links to the notice; the
original article remains available. In the rare case of a retraction, the article
is not deleted: a dated retraction notice stating the reason is published and
bidirectionally linked with the article, and the article and its PDF are clearly
marked as retracted. This follows the conventions of the Committee on Publication
Ethics (COPE).

## Citation style

References follow IEEE numeric style, numbered in order of first appearance and
cited in text as [1], [2]. Each entry gives the author(s) as initials plus
surname, the title in quotes, the venue, the year, and a DOI or stable URL; web
sources additionally give an access date. Sources should be primary and
verifiable -- peer-reviewed papers, standards, official documentation, patents,
and recognized grey literature. Question-and-answer threads, wikis, and vendor
marketing or glossary pages are not acceptable as the sole support for a
technical claim.
